Section 01

Where a seat can come from

NEET gives you a rank. It does not give you a college. Counselling is the separate step where your rank is matched to a seat — and there are five places that seat can come from.

Rank → eligibility

Your rank sets the doors you may knock on.

Choices → outcome

Your ordered choices decide which one opens.

Reporting → yours

Only reporting in person locks the seat.

The five sources of a seat

All India Quota — 15%

MCC · ANY STATE · PURE RANK

15% of government-college seats, filled by the Medical Counselling Committee. Students from every state compete purely on All-India Rank — no domicile needed.

State Quota — 85%

STATE AUTHORITY · DOMICILE

85% of government-college seats are reserved for the state's own candidates. Each state runs its own counselling, with its own rules on domicile, nativity and schooling.

Deemed Universities

MCC · ALL STATES ELIGIBLE

Deemed medical universities are filled through centralised MCC counselling under the Deemed category. Fees run higher, but candidates from any state may apply.

Private Universities

UNDER THE STATE

Private colleges sit under the State, run mostly through state or state-private universities. Tuition varies widely and is usually well above government colleges.

AIIMS · JIPMER · Central Institutes of National Importance

Admission to all AIIMS campuses, JIPMER, and other Institutes of National Importance runs only through NEET UG and centralised counselling. These are among the most competitive seats in the country.

Remember — NEET ≠ admission, and counselling ≠ admission either. A rank earns a place in the queue; an allotment offers a seat; only reporting confirms it. And one exam feeds many counsellings — you can take part in several at once.

One exam body, two counselling engines. Knowing who runs what tells you where to register.

  • 15% All-India Quota of state government institutes
  • Central government institutes — AIIMS, JIPMER, AMU, IP, DU

  • 85% state quota of state government institutes
  • 100% of seats in state private institutes / universities

The eight stages that decide everything

From result day to your first class, All-India counselling moves through eight stages. Miss a window and the map redraws itself.

1

Result day

Download your scorecard & rank letter. Note your All-India rank and state merit rank separately, and confirm your category.

2

Registration

Register on the portal, upload nativity / community certificates, and pay the refundable security deposit. Register for AIQ too — more chances.

3

Choice filling

The step that decides everything. Add 25–30+ colleges, ordered by real preference. Check fees and location for each.

4

Allotment · Rounds 1 & 2

Hold a seat while staying eligible to upgrade. A better allotment auto-replaces the old one. Surrendering after Round 2 forfeits the deposit.

5

Classes begin & upgradation

Opt for upgrade if you want a better college; a new seat auto-cancels the old. No upgrade means you keep your current seat.

6

Mop-up & stray vacancy

The last opening for those still without a seat. Same process as earlier rounds — don't wait to confirm.

7

Reporting to college

The seat becomes official. Report in person within the deadline with nativity, community and NEET originals, and finish admission formalities.

8

Course commences

Your medical journey begins. Join on the scheduled date, complete remaining formalities, and stay on top of academic notices.

~24hr

typical window to respond to an allotment — no extensions

~3 days

you may get between allotment and reporting — keep documents ready

3+

rounds plus mop-up & stray — each with its own rules

The tell — downloading your allotment order is often treated as having joined. Read each round's rules before you click anything.

Two pathways, one vocabulary

Counselling runs on two parallel tracks and a handful of words you'll see everywhere. Learn both before you register.

MCC counselling
  • Covers the 15% All-India Quota seats
  • AIIMS, JIPMER, central universities & deemed universities
  • One national portal, one national schedule
State counselling
  • Covers the 85% state quota seats
  • Run separately by each state's own authority
  • Its own portal, rules, dates and deposit

Terms you must know

Seat matrix

The availability chart — how many seats each college and course holds for each category. Read it first; it shows where the opportunities actually are before you make a single choice.

Choice list

Your ranked wishlist of colleges and courses, ordered from "I'd love this" down to "I'd still be happy with this." The system reads it top to bottom.

Choice filling

Telling the system what you want, in order. Simple rule: put your true favourite at #1, because preferences are honoured strictly in the sequence you set.

Seat allotment

The moment choices turn into a seat. You'll see the college and course offered; accept it, or if eligible, stay in for a later round to upgrade.

Before counselling begins — the prep list

  • Download scorecard + rank letter on result day; print several copies.
  • Map your AIQ rank against last year's closing ranks — mark safe, target and dream colleges.
  • Confirm your category: UR / OBC-NCL / SC / ST / EWS / PwD / Defence.
  • Renew OBC-NCL & EWS certificates if needed (issued in the current cycle).
  • Build a target list across AIQ and your home state; add 2–3 open states if useful.
  • Organise every document before allotment — you may get only ~3 working days to report.

Remember — a good outcome isn't just your NEET score. It's making informed choices and applying through the right channels. The rank opens doors; the strategy decides which one you walk through.

Section 07

Rules that catch people out

Most counselling regrets come from a handful of hard rules. Read these once, carefully.

Choice locking is final

Locked choices can't be changed for any reason — no exceptions, no extensions.

Deadlines are unforgiving

Not resigning on time can confirm your admission; not reporting on time can cancel your seat.

No stray round while holding a seat

Holding any AIQ seat automatically bars you from the state stray-vacancy round.

OBC-NCL must be current

An expired certificate can push you into the UR category during verification.

Deposit loss on deemed exit

Resigning a deemed-university seat after joining via Round 2 forfeits the ₹2 lakh security deposit.

No category reservation in deemed

SC/ST/OBC/EWS/PwD reservation does not apply to deemed universities under All-India counselling.

Key decisions during counselling

Round 1 — accept or resign?

Accept & try to upgrade is almost always right: your seat is held safely; if Round 2 gives better, it auto-cancels; if not, you keep it.

Resign to go fresh only if you'd never attend that college — high risk, as later rounds may have fewer seats and stiffer competition.

AIQ seat vs state seat

Govt AIQ vs govt state: pick the stronger college by reputation, not location. AIQ is harder to get, but your home state may have the better institute.

Govt vs private: take the government seat almost every time — unless the private college is genuinely in the CMC Vellore / St John's league.

Deemed seat — join or wait?

Deemed fees are often far higher. If you have a realistic shot at a government college through state counselling, it can be worth waiting before you commit.

Drop year — worth it?

Makes sense if you're borderline govt/private, missed by ≤50 marks, targeting top AIIMS, and family can absorb the delay.

Doesn't if you're already above a decent govt closing rank, already dropped once, or scoring 600+ with little room to climb.

Good to know

PwD's 5% reservation is horizontal — it doesn't add new seats. It ensures 5% of existing UR/OBC/EWS/SC/ST seats go to PwD candidates within each category.

Which quota changes everything

Not all seats are equal. The quota a seat belongs to decides who can take it, how it's allotted, and what it costs.

15% All-India Quota (AIQ)
  • Open to students from every state; run by MCC
  • Allotted purely on All-India Rank — no domicile preference
  • Applies only to government colleges

e.g. a Tamil Nadu student can win a government seat in another state on a strong rank.

85% State Quota
  • Reserved mainly for the state's own students
  • Allotted on the state merit list from NEET scores
  • Applies to government & private colleges

e.g. a Tamil Nadu student securing a government seat within TN via the 85% quota.

Management Quota (private)
  • Private-college seats at higher fees
  • Open to all NEET-qualified students, no domicile bar
  • Available only in private medical colleges

e.g. a lower rank can still secure a private seat if the family can meet the fees.

NRI Quota
  • For NRI students or those sponsored by an NRI
  • Fees significantly higher than other quotas
  • Available in private & deemed universities

e.g. a student with an NRI sponsor may secure a seat even on a lower rank.

Registration to reporting — do's & don'ts

Do

Apply for nativity & community certificates early — start now to avoid last-minute delays.

Register for both AIQ and state — two pathways raise your odds.

Research colleges before the window — prep lets you decide fast and calm.

Rank your true first choice at #1 — preferences are honoured in order.

Set alerts for every result date — windows are short and strict.

Don't

List only 2–3 colleges — a short list can leave you with no seat.

Ignore state-specific rules — some treat post-allotment actions as acceptance.

Surrender after Round 2 — you may lose your deposit and face restrictions.

Ignore name mismatches — even small differences stall verification.

Skip the bond terms — some colleges carry long service commitments or heavy penalties.

One line to live by — more well-chosen choices, filed on time, with clean documents. That combination protects you at every round.

Quick answers to common doubts

The questions nearly every student and parent asks, answered plainly.

1

Can I take part in both AIQ and state counselling at once?

Yes. MCC (All-India) and state counselling run simultaneously and independently. Register for both and compare allotments before deciding.

2

What if I don't respond to an allotment in time?

Your seat is cancelled and you may lose the right to that round, depending on its rules. The response window is typically about 24 hours, with no extensions.

3

What's the difference between freeze and float (upgrade)?

Freezing means accepting and keeping your current allotment. Floating (upgrade) means staying eligible for a higher-preference seat in later rounds, as the authority permits.

4

Will I lose my security deposit if I withdraw?

It depends on the counselling. In most, the deposit is forfeited if you don't join or if you resign after a Round 2 (or later) allotment.

5

Can I change my community certificate after registration?

No. The community category submitted at registration is final. Make sure it's correct before you submit.

6

Do I register separately for each state?

Yes. Each state has its own authority, registration process, eligibility rules and schedule.

7

How many colleges should I add to my choice list?

Aim for 30–50. More choices mean more safety; a short list risks no seat at all if your rank misses the few you listed.

8

Can a seat be lost over missing documents?

Yes — even after allotment. Verification matters as much as rank. Missing, expired or incorrect certificates (category, domicile, eligibility) can cancel an allotment.

9

Is AIQ only for top rankers?

No. All-India counselling covers thousands of seats across government colleges, AIIMS, central and deemed universities, spanning many rank brackets and categories.

10

Can I leave an AIQ seat for a better option later?

Usually yes, but timing matters. Depending on the round and rules, you may upgrade or shift — though deadlines and resignation policies can affect eligibility and deposits.
